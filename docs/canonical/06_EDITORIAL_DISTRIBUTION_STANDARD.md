## 1. Purpose

This Standard governs how BaseballOS communicates trustworthy bullpen intelligence on the product, through immutable share artifacts, and across external channels.

The Bullpen Intelligence Standard decides what may be claimed. The Product Experience Standard decides the destination experience. This Standard decides how the claim is written, packaged, adapted, distributed, corrected, and measured.

## 2. Editorial Mission

BaseballOS communicates trustworthy bullpen intelligence in a way that a baseball fan can understand, repeat, and verify.

The product should feel like:

- a careful baseball person noticing something worth discussing;
- a builder willing to show the work;
- a source that is confident about what it knows and direct about what it does not;
- a niche product with one clear purpose.

It should not feel like a sportsbook, hype account, generic analytics brand, automated content mill, or defensive legal disclaimer.

## 3. The Content Test

Before publishing, ask:

> Would a baseball fan understand the baseball point even if they never click BaseballOS?

If no, the content is promotional rather than useful and should be rewritten.

Every publication owns one primary point. A post, card, email, or article may include supporting context, but it should not attempt to explain the entire platform, league, methodology, trust system, and multiple unrelated storylines at once.

## 4. Voice Traits

| Trait | Rule |
|---|---|
| Direct | Say what happened or what was decided without a long setup |
| Curious | Invite the reader to inspect the observation rather than command a conclusion |
| Grounded | Use one specific baseball or engineering example |
| Reflective | Explain what a decision, mistake, or observation taught the founder |
| Confident, not loud | Stand behind evidence without exaggerating certainty |
| Builder-first | Write from the perspective of the person making the tradeoffs |
| Plainspoken | Prefer ordinary baseball language over product jargon |
| Checkable | Put dates, windows, names, and receipts close to the claim |

### Founder voice

Use first person for founder experience: I built, I noticed, I changed, I was wrong, I removed, I learned.

### Product voice

Use BaseballOS for public behavior and claims: BaseballOS shows, classifies, withholds, corrected, or published.

Avoid vague corporate language that hides a solo-built product.

## 5. Default Writing Rules

- Use contractions when natural.
- Use short paragraphs.
- Lead with the human tension, baseball observation, or engineering decision.
- Introduce the technology stack only after the reader understands why the work matters.
- Use one memorable example instead of several weaker examples.
- Acknowledge uncertainty directly.
- Prefer a specific date/window over recently or lately.
- Do not open with "Excited to announce."
- Do not close with a generic "check it out."
- Let the product mention explain the source or decision rather than become the point.
- Never add AI attribution to public posts, artifacts, repository records, or bylines.

## 6. Phrases to Avoid

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Game-changing / revolutionary | Name the exact improvement and who notices it |
| A cool project | An MLB bullpen-intelligence platform, or the exact capability |
| Based on our analysis | State the source facts and window |
| The bullpen will struggle tonight | The bullpen has carried a heavier recent workload |
| The manager clearly intends | Describe completed deployment history |
| AI-powered | Mention automation only when genuine, explainable, and relevant |
| Check it out | End with the baseball point, lesson, or specific question |
| The system says | BaseballOS published X because Y evidence passed |
| The bullpen edge | The observable difference between the two bullpen states |
| Needs a day off | Has pitched in five of the last seven games |
| An elite / dominant / terrible bullpen | State the metric, group, sample, and window and stop |
| A trustworthy bullpen | Describe the recorded results and the arms who produced them |

## 7. Content Pillars

Every publication uses one primary pillar.

### P1 - Founder Judgment

**Purpose:** build trust in the person making product decisions.

Best topics: choosing a narrow category, removing features, refusing prediction, deleting code, learning from a bug, changing a source-authority rule, balancing ambition with limited capacity, choosing trust over reach.

Best home: LinkedIn, with occasional newsletter or site expansion.

Success signal: relevant professional conversation, profile visits, credible connections, and recognition of Nickolis as a thoughtful builder.

### P2 - Bullpen Insight

**Purpose:** establish BaseballOS as a useful source of current MLB bullpen understanding.

Best topics: short starts and innings transferred to the bullpen, workload accumulation, named concentration, active-option changes, recovery after an off-day, roster churn, current active-pen performance, a descriptive comparison, and what changed after the latest completed game.

Best homes: X, Instagram, Reddit, newsletter, team communities, and the BaseballOS site.

Success signal: shares, saves, knowledgeable replies, evidence inspection, team-page visits, and citations.

### P3 - Technical Trust

**Purpose:** show how engineering choices make the baseball explanation more reliable.

Best topics: fail-closed publication, official starter authority, appearance-ledger audits, immutable artifacts, correction workflows, unknown values staying unknown, tests that catch baseball errors, why an internal score was removed, and source provenance.

Best homes: LinkedIn, site articles, newsletter, and occasional X threads.

Success signal: technical discussion, repository/portfolio interest, recruiter or sports-tech engagement, and trust-page visits.

### P4 - Evergreen Explainer

**Purpose:** create durable reference material that compounds through search, internal links, and repeated social use.

Best topics: what makes a bullpen taxed or stretched, how reliever workload is measured, why pitch count matters more than appearance count alone, how short starts affect a bullpen, what bullpen concentration means, what roster status changes about the available group, why state and performance are different, and what BaseballOS cannot see.

Best home: BaseballOS site with condensed native versions elsewhere.

Success signal: search traffic, long-tail visits, backlinks, internal navigation, and repeated citation.

## 8. Universal Publication Contract

Every publication preserves this meaning:

```text
Hook
-> State
-> Why
-> Evidence
-> Freshness
-> Limitation
-> Conversation or takeaway
```

Not every small post requires a separate sentence for each layer, but no material layer may disappear.

### Hook

Approved hooks include a surprising baseball contrast, difficult founder decision, visible mistake/correction, common assumption complicated by evidence, timely game/series context, clear user problem, or trustworthy refusal.

A hook may create curiosity. It may not promise an outcome the evidence cannot support.

### State

State the baseball point in plain language.

Good: "The Yankees bullpen enters tonight Stretched after covering 19.1 innings across four games."

Weak: "The Yankees have a bullpen pressure score of 72."

The first is understandable and checkable. The second asks the reader to trust an abstraction.

### Why

Explain the observable mechanism: starter exited early, same three arms carried recent work, active group absorbed added coverage, repeated-day use, roster change reduced depth, or an off-day reopened the window.

Do not replace observable history with manager intent or health inference.

### Evidence

Every substantive claim needs a receipt. Approved receipts include appearance dates, game IDs/opponents, pitches, outs/innings, batters faced, rest days, rolling window, active-arm counts, named concentration, official roster transaction, completed starter-length range, current active-pen performance with sample definition, and immutable artifact evidence.

One to three strong receipts usually beat six small numbers.

### Freshness

Every time-sensitive publication visibly states date/data-through, window, rolling window, product date, game status when relevant, and generated/published time for an artifact.

A screenshot should preserve its freshness stamp. When a crop removes it, the caption must restate it.

### Limitation

State a limitation when it materially affects interpretation.

Good: "This describes recorded game workload; BaseballOS cannot see bullpen warm-ups or private manager plans."

Bad: "Not advice. Results may vary."

Limitations protect the claim; they should not make the product sound afraid to say anything.

### Conversation or takeaway

End with one specific baseball question, one product/engineering lesson, one factual next event in the observable record, or a complete insight that needs no prompt.

Avoid engagement bait.

## 9. Permanent Content Boundaries

- No picks, predictions, odds, betting advice, or edge framing.
- No fantasy start/sit claims.
- No private injury claims.
- No certainty about manager decisions or future usage.
- No rankings, grades, or winner verdicts between bullpens.
- No quality adjective earned from a performance metric alone.
- No performance value published without its group, sample, and represented date.
- No unexplained black-box numbers.
- No unknown values converted to zero.
- No stale, partial, fallback, or sample data presented as current.
- No artificial controversy.
- No manipulated screenshot that removes material evidence or date context.
- No AI attribution in public copy or repository authorship.

## 10. Quiet-Day Rule

A quiet day may produce no post.

Do not publish merely to satisfy a calendar when there is no strong observation, source data is incomplete, the point was made recently, the post would be generic, a material limitation overwhelms the claim, or the game already made the planned pregame frame stale.

Publishing less is better than teaching the audience to ignore BaseballOS.

## 11. Topic Clarity and Discoverability

Across the body of work, BaseballOS should naturally discuss MLB bullpen analysis, bullpen workload, reliever usage/rest, bullpen management, high-leverage relievers, short starts and innings coverage, active bullpen performance, fresh/stretched/vulnerable bullpens, evidence-first public data, and source authority.

Use established baseball terms naturally. Use one primary BaseballOS phrase and zero or one distinctive brand term in a typical post. Mention technical terms only when the content is genuinely technical.

Do not keyword-stuff. Search is earned through useful answers, stable destinations, clear titles, structured content, and internal links.

## 12A. Performance Language

A performance metric governed by the Current Active-Pen Performance Contract describes recorded results by a named group over an approved window. Copy must stay descriptive.

A material performance claim carries, adjacent to the number:

- the group - whose results these are, and how many arms are in it;
- the sample - the qualifying appearances or innings behind the value;
- the represented date and window;
- at least one inspectable receipt or the exact destination that reaches one.

A performance value with no group and no sample beside it is not publishable, in a post, a caption, a card, a newsletter, or alt text.

Prohibited on the basis of a performance metric alone: elite, dominant, terrible, trustworthy, untrustworthy, best, worst, collapsing, lights-out, and any grade, tier, or ranking verdict. A single-metric value never earns a quality judgment about a bullpen or a pitcher.

Permitted: "Their current active pen has allowed 31 earned runs across 96.1 relief innings this season, across the eight arms in the group as of July 28." That is checkable. "Their bullpen has been elite" is not.

State is not performance, and copy may not blend them. A bullpen described as Stretched has not been described as bad, and a bullpen with strong recorded results has not been described as rested.

Below an approved minimum sample there is no number to write about. Use the approved wording — **Not Enough Innings Yet** — with the group's current innings and the required innings beside it. Do not fill the gap with a prior value, a league figure, or an adjective, and do not substitute an arm-read label such as Limited Read or Unavailable, which mean something else about a single pitcher.

The approved public performance terms are exactly **Active Bullpen ERA** and **Not Enough Innings Yet**. Copy uses them as written. It does not shorten Active Bullpen ERA to "bullpen ERA," which names a different number, and it does not invent a third term.

Two worked lines, both publishable:

> Cincinnati's Active Bullpen ERA is 2.90 — the eight arms in the pen as of July 30, over 96.1 relief innings for the Reds this season.

> Not Enough Innings Yet for Cincinnati's Active Bullpen ERA: this group has thrown 22.0 relief innings for the Reds, and 36.0 are required before the number publishes.

When the contributing-arm count is materially smaller than the group size, copy says so — "six of the eight have pitched for them this season" — rather than letting the group size imply a sample no one threw.

Include the family limitation when it materially affects interpretation, in one sentence, near the claim - not as a recurring block of defensive language.

## 13. Hashtags

- Default to none on X.
- Use zero to three on LinkedIn only when they clarify the subject.
- Use a small relevant set on Instagram in the caption, not a detached spam block.
- Never let hashtags replace precise nouns.
- Event tags may be used when an active baseball event genuinely improves discovery.

## 14. LinkedIn

**Primary job:** build professional credibility around Nickolis as a developer, product thinker, and baseball-technology builder.

Default structure:

1. Open with a belief, mistake, decision, or tension.
2. Explain the baseball/product problem.
3. Give one concrete example.
4. Explain what BaseballOS revealed or changed.
5. Add technical context only after the point is clear.
6. End with the lesson or one specific question.

Working length: usually 150 to 300 words in short paragraphs. This is a practical target, not an algorithm rule.

Rules:

- baseball example before technology list;
- no resume dump;
- no inflated startup language;
- use first person;
- mention BaseballOS once or twice where natural;
- prefer a decision/lesson over a feature catalog;
- attach a screenshot only when it carries real evidence or architecture meaning.

## 15. X

**Primary job:** join timely baseball discussion with one specific observation and immediately visible evidence.

Default structure:

- name the team/player;
- state one sharp fact;
- provide one compact receipt or evidence-bearing artifact;
- include freshness;
- omit filler.

Rules:

- timeliness matters;
- a post should stand alone even with a link;
- use a short thread only when evidence genuinely needs it;
- default to zero hashtags;
- do not vague-post about "a bullpen" when the team can be named;
- do not quote-tweet a team/fan merely to advertise BaseballOS;
- helpful replies are part of distribution;
- respect current character limits and verify before publishing.

## 16. Instagram

**Primary job:** help non-followers understand one bullpen idea quickly through a strong original visual.

Preferred formats:

- carousel for teaching or evidence sequence;
- Reel only when the point can be explained clearly on video;
- single image for one strong immutable artifact.

Carousel structure:

1. unmistakable first-frame point;
2. current state and team identity;
3. named evidence;
4. why the context matters;
5. freshness, limitation, and exact destination.

Rules:

- the first frame communicates the point immediately;
- text remains legible in-feed;
- use original evidence-bearing visuals;
- avoid generic stadium/player AI art;
- do not create a text-slide carousel from one weak fact;
- place relevant hashtags in the caption;
- captions remain readable and conversational.

## 17. Reddit

**Primary job:** contribute to a team/baseball community without treating the subreddit as a distribution channel.

Default structure: factual community-specific title, what was noticed, minimum evidence, honest uncertainty, and a genuine question for people who watch the team.

Rules:

- read subreddit rules before every post;
- community value before brand mention;
- title remains factual/team-specific;
- screenshots support discussion;
- correct community members respectfully and update errors promptly;
- let people ask for the source when direct promotion is not appropriate.

## 18. Newsletter

**Primary job:** give subscribers a dependable reason to return.

Manual weekly briefing structure:

- one main finding;
- one explanation;
- one evidence block;
- one limitation/currentness line;
- one exact deeper-view link;
- optional short founder note.

A manual ritual must prove open, read, and click behavior before automation becomes a product project.

## 19. BaseballOS Site and Search

Evergreen pages should answer recurring bullpen questions completely enough to remain useful after social conversation ends.

Structure: precise title, direct answer, definition of key term, original examples/evidence, methodology, freshness/limitations, related pages/glossary/trust links, visible authorship, and structured data only when the page truly fits the schema.

Do not create thin keyword pages, let internal links flow into product intent/model details, or let archived share pages replace current team pages as evergreen destinations.

## 20. Creator and Analyst Outreach

**Primary job:** offer a useful source, not request free promotion.

Structure:

1. personal, specific reason for contacting the person;
2. one-sentence BaseballOS description;
3. one example relevant to their work/team/audience;
4. exact evidence/artifact link;
5. low-pressure invitation to look, respond, or offer feedback;
6. no generic media-kit attachment unless it directly helps.

Do not mass-send identical messages, claim partnership or endorsement, ask for a post in the first message, make correction easy and welcome, and track conversations rather than an abstract response rate.

## 21. Share Artifacts and Visual Distribution

BaseballOS does not share graphics. It shares verifiable baseball intelligence.

The image is one rendering of an immutable canonical artifact. The artifact owns claim, evidence, freshness, limitation, alt text, description, and destination.

### Team State artifact hierarchy

1. full team identity;
2. BaseballOS brand;
3. public Team State;
4. one why sentence;
5. two to four evidence receipts;
6. data-through/updated line;
7. compact trust/source line;
8. exact share destination.

A card without evidence and freshness is a rumor with a logo.

### Artifact copy rules

- state-first and factual;
- full team name, no ambiguous city-only name;
- no internal enum or model output;
- no naked score;
- no future result;
- no manager certainty;
- no custom user-edited numeric claim in V1;
- no manually retyped second number to force an ineligible card;
- caption adaptation preserves the artifact's meaning.

### Visual standard

- primary portrait export 1080 by 1350 pixels;
- responsive web card from the same information hierarchy;
- deep navy/charcoal background;
- high-contrast off-white text;
- restrained blue and muted gold accents;
- condensed display face for state/headline, readable sans serif for evidence;
- photo-free by default;
- no player likenesses, fake team marks, cinematic tunnels, smoke, sparks, decorative baseball clichés, or generic AI art;
- state is communicated by text, not color alone;
- freshness remains legible in crop and preview;
- authoritative alt text is stored with the artifact.

Every external card links to its exact `/share/{public_id}` page, never only to the generic homepage.

### Correction workflow

When a published artifact is materially wrong:

1. stop distributing it;
2. create corrected replacement from governed source state;
3. mark original superseded or withdrawn with plain-language reason;
4. link original and replacement;
5. preserve original for accountability;
6. use replacement in future distribution;
7. publish correction when the original reached an audience.

## 22. Cadence and Repurposing

Default sustainable ceiling:

- no more than three primary social publications per week;
- no more than one weekly newsletter/briefing when the manual ritual is active.

A launch/postseason campaign may temporarily allocate those slots to one platform. It must not create a second unsustainable cadence.

Replies, helpful comments, and native adaptations of the same source insight are encouraged when useful and not separate spam.

One source insight may become:

- an X observation plus artifact;
- an Instagram evidence carousel;
- a LinkedIn founder/product lesson about why comfortable final scores can hide bullpen cost;
- a newsletter briefing with receipts;
- an evergreen site article or team page.

Repurpose the research, not the wording. Do not copy a caption everywhere and ignore platform behavior.

## 23. Working Weekly Rhythm

A sustainable example:

- one current Bullpen Insight;
- one Founder Judgment or Technical Trust post;
- one second Bullpen Insight or Evergreen explainer adaptation;
- one weekly briefing only when manual proof exists;
- targeted replies/comments where BaseballOS adds real context.

The Roadmap may temporarily change the allocation. The ceiling and quality standard remain.

## 24. Drafting and Approval Workflow

A raw brief contains platform, audience, one point, source facts or artifact, data-through date, what feels interesting, length/format constraint, and whether the goal is discussion, education, professional credibility, or distribution.

Editorial assistance returns recommended pillar/goal, up to three hook options in Nickolis's voice, one final platform-native draft, primary topic phrase/optional BaseballOS phrase, trust check, cross-platform adaptations only where useful, and no missing-evidence claim disguised as better copy.

Nickolis owns final wording and authorship. Before publishing, ask:

> Would I say this out loud?

If it sounds unlike him, simplify it.

### Evidence verification

Before writing a current baseball claim:

1. confirm source authority;
2. verify exact team/player identity;
3. verify completed-game finality;
4. verify official starter/reliever distinction;
5. verify values and units;
6. verify window/data-through date;
7. verify active group/roster definition for performance metrics;
8. use immutable artifact when available;
9. do not draft around unresolved discrepancies.

## 25. Corrections and Public Accountability

Correct material errors plainly and quickly.

A correction states what was wrong, what the authoritative record shows, whether the original conclusion changed, and the correct date/window. It does not delete the mistake and pretend it never published.

| Severity | Example | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Minor presentation | Typo that does not change baseball meaning | Edit where channel permits; no correction post required |
| Material fact | Wrong pitches, innings, date, roster state, starter, or team | Correct original; publish correction if distributed; replace artifact |
| Material conclusion | Evidence error changed Fresh/Stretched/Vulnerable or story | Stop distribution; supersede/withdraw; explain correction |
| Trust/system incident | Multiple outputs affected | Public scope statement plus technical correction record when appropriate |

Tone: direct, factual, no defensiveness, no blame shifting, no exaggerated apology, and explanation of prevention only after the corrected fact.

Example:

> Correction: BaseballOS originally described Ferguson as the starter in this game. The official box score lists Burns as the starter. The appearance record and dependent bullpen evidence have been corrected; the original artifact has been superseded.

Use real names/details only after verification.

## 26. Measurement

Likes are not a universal success metric. Measure the job of the pillar/channel.

| Job | Stronger signals |
|---|---|
| Founder Judgment | Relevant comments, profile visits, connections, recruiter/sports-tech interest |
| Bullpen Insight | Shares, saves, knowledgeable replies, evidence clicks, team-page visits, citations |
| Technical Trust | Methodology/trust visits, technical discussion, repository/portfolio interest |
| Evergreen Explainer | Search impressions, long-tail visits, backlinks, internal navigation |
| Share Artifact | Link/image actions, human landing views, evidence inspection, downstream opens |
| Newsletter | Open/read/click/reply quality and return behavior |
| Outreach | Relevant replies, follow-up conversations, unsolicited use/citation |

Review windows:

- 24 hours: initial distribution and conversation quality;
- 72 hours: profile visits, follows, saves, shares, and secondary distribution;
- 7 days: traffic, subscribers, relevant connections, backlinks, and long-tail reach;
- 30 days: pillar mix, sustainable cadence, topic clarity, returning behavior, and evidence-inspection patterns.

Do not rewrite permanent trust rules because one aggressive post performed well.

## 28. Universal Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] One point owns the publication.
- [ ] Primary audience and pillar are defined.
- [ ] Hook creates real tension/clarity without hype.
- [ ] Baseball point is useful without a click.
- [ ] At least one specific receipt is present.
- [ ] Team/player/game identity is verified.
- [ ] Official starter/reliever and appearance facts are correct.
- [ ] Values, units, window, and date-through are verified.
- [ ] Limitation is included only where material.
- [ ] No prediction, betting, fantasy, ranking, private health, or manager-intent claim appears.
- [ ] No internal enum, black-box score, or unknown-as-zero appears.
- [ ] Screenshot/card preserves freshness.
- [ ] Exact destination is used.
- [ ] Wording sounds like Nickolis.
- [ ] Ending is a real question, useful lesson, or complete insight.
